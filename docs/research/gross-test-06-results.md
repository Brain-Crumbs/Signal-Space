## Error budget and limits

The selected core uses $R=80m^{-1}$ and $h=0.1m^{-1}$, with $h/2$ and $2R$ checked independently. The main evolution lasts 100 periods; the grid/domain and nonnominal amplitude evolutions last 20 periods. Those shorter controls constrain eigenfrequency and early response but **do not independently verify a 100-period continuum lifetime**. The outer 20 radial units have a logged momentum damping layer.

The nominal outer energy sink is $3.51\times10^{-5}$, while the clock mode begins with only $6.74\times10^{-6}$ energy units. The total-energy-plus-sink diagnostic residual is $9.04\times10^{-5}$, or **13.4 initial clock-mode energies** (although small compared with core energy). Thus the run records a sink but cannot isolate clock radiation energy from background numerical energy at the clock scale. The near-constant projected mode and trace support this **finite radial clock usability** result; they do not supply a resolved emission spectrum.

The two lower-frequency BVP seeds failed or collapsed to the trivial solution; continuation also lost the nontrivial branch. This is a numerical limitation, not a claim that Q-balls do not exist there. A thin-wall calculation may need a larger domain and an arclength or constrained continuation formulation. Nonspherical perturbations, nonlinear fragmentation, finite gravity, observer-frame comparisons, marker coupling and neutral reception are untested.
