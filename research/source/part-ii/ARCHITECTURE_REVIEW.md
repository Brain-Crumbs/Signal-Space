# Signal Space architecture review for Foundations Part II

11 September 2026 | Review accompanying version 0.1.0

## Assessment

The full-theory architecture remains a coherent research program because it separates quantum composition, operational geometry, autonomous dynamics, gravitational response, matter spectra, and constants into explicit bridges. Its most valuable constraint is that the final explanations must coexist within one dynamics. The main unresolved issue is selecting that dynamics; neither Part I nor this Part II supplies it.

The completed Part I is *Composition of Signal Histories*, version 0.1.0, dated 11 September 2026. I recovered and reviewed its actual Markdown source. It adopts quantum structure explicitly and establishes a finite operational record framework. This review does not treat the much earlier Signal Space Paper III as the preceding part of the new series. That earlier paper is retained as a source of clock-calibration and memory constraints.

Part II now develops the publication-plan item *Signal States, Lorentz Cones and Operational Clocks*. Its defensible contribution is a conditional mathematical bridge and a set of discriminating controls. It is not yet a reconstruction of physical spacetime from autonomous reception dynamics. “Publication-ready” here refers to a complete, referenced manuscript with explicit proofs and reproducible calculations; journal acceptance, priority of results, and empirical support are not established.

## What the source architecture gets right

1. **It retains the cost of the quantum postulate.** Part I’s operational composition is useful precisely because its Hilbert spaces, instruments, and trace rule are declared. This paper inherits them and does not derive them a second time from positive rates.
2. **It proposes a complete cone rather than arbitrary angular moments.** Positive Hermitian two-by-two matrices have the exact determinant and group action needed for Lorentz algebra. This is more specific than assigning four-vector behavior to a few unrelated statistical summaries.
3. **It makes calibration independent of reconstruction.** The earlier stationary count identity is a benchmark, while the transient discrepancy is a constraint. A replacement clock must have a physical recurrence and a separately fixed calibration.
4. **It postpones universal claims until one model supports them.** Geometry reconstruction, vacuum selection, mass-gap analysis, and constants cannot be completed by stitching together independently tuned examples.
5. **It gives failure branches.** Intensity matrices can remain internal-state variables if their geometric interpretation fails; phase clocks can be replaced if their response is not controlled; Euclidean or algebraic routes remain alternatives rather than unsupported extra sectors.

## New points that sharpen the architecture

**The positive matrix has a useful operational meaning that is too broad to be geometric by itself.** It can encode an ordinary qubit ensemble times intensity. Recording changes its determinant at fixed diagonal populations; attenuation changes its scale; a stationary pure preparation lies on its null boundary. None requires motion. Any physical spacetime interpretation needs an additional comparison law that survives these controls.

**Nonunitary covariance needs a measure and normalization story.** A dual transformation preserves the bilinear intensity-effect pairing, but an ordinary POVM no longer sums to the unchanged identity under a nonunitary inverse congruence. A consistent observer description must transform the relevant exposure, density, or normalization structure. Calling the operation a passive frame change is not enough.

**The original count-clock target contains a nonlinear averaging operation.** Proper time of the ensemble-mean curve is not the mean proper time of constituent curves. Fixed local residence and jump rewards have expectation affine in the directional mean, so they cannot equal the square-root target for all preparations. This is a restricted no-go statement, not a prohibition of clocks or quantum phases. It makes the case for a bound composite with its own physical center more precise.

**The cavity result supports the phase-clock alternative only at benchmark level.** A symmetric ideal cavity under constant proper acceleration has a calculable quadratic size correction under fixed inertial calibration. That is useful progress beyond assuming a phase law proportional to proper time. Yet the field, boundaries, and acceleration are prescribed, and its center is not the individual realization of the Markov process. The manuscript keeps those construction families separate.

**The simplest modular identification fails directly.** For a faithful two-level state, real modular flow is unitary conjugation about its polarization axis. It is not positive boost congruence. The richer modular proposal remains possible in a localized continuum algebra, but its successful regime must be demonstrated.

## Recommended changes to the research plan

Retain the architecture and its publication titles. Add four explicit acceptance conditions to the operational-geometry stages:

1. A signal operator must declare its units, exposure, accessible protocol class, and relation to retained memory before any spacetime interpretation.
2. A frame law must predict a spanning collection of preparations with one transformation and a consistent normalization, including detector and source controls.
3. Clock claims must name the trajectory being timed, distinguish individual composite motion from ensemble-mean curves, and include fixed calibration plus calculable device errors.
4. The finite two-component modular flow must be treated as an obstruction/control for the proposed continuum modular-boost bridge, rather than evidence for that bridge.

No additional fundamental particle topology is needed at this stage. Do not equate the cavity’s finite-volume mode spacing with a mass gap or its symmetric boundaries with a self-bound particle. Both would add claims beyond the calculation.

## Concrete handoff to Part III

Select one local regulated evolution, including all memory and whatever routing or connectivity is dynamical. State its independent parameters and the state-preparation or vacuum-selection rule. Before attempting Einstein dynamics, ask whether that single evolution has both propagating modes and bounded recurrent subsystems with controlled exchange records.

The first useful decision is whether the selected model can produce a common propagation cone and a recurrent phase frequency without assigning unrelated update laws to each. Measure regulator anisotropy, mode-dependent limiting speeds, size dependence, and response to perturbations. Compare its clock law against the exact count and cavity controls in Part II. If these require independent tuning for every sector, reject or narrow the candidate before moving to constants.

Part II’s formulas provide tests and interfaces for that study. They do not prescribe a unique candidate or claim that one has already been selected.
